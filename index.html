The problem

A spec sheet can't show a buyer how a gearbox works.

  1. 01

    Specs don't explain function

    Buyers who don't understand the mechanism end up comparing on price. Datasheets list numbers; they rarely show why your design is better.

  2. 02

    Videos only go one way

    A video plays the same for everyone. A buyer can't stop at the part they care about, look inside it or ask about it.

  3. 03

    Stand visitors disappear

    Visitors take a flyer and walk on. You don't know what caught their interest, and most never get in touch.

The product

What a buyer would do with the real machine, in a browser tab.

The Interactive Equipment Explainer is built for mechanical products: assemblies with parts that move, mesh, clamp or spin.

Click any part

The camera flies to it and a panel explains what it does, with specs and a button to ask about it.

X-ray, section, blueprint

See through the housing, cut it open or switch to a technical-drawing look.

Exploded view and assembly

Pull the assembly apart along its real axes, then put it back together step by step.

Guided walkthroughs

A captioned sequence shows the working cycle: how the brake clamps, how the reduction works.

Live parameters

Sliders for speed or load, with read-outs that update while the model runs.

Colours and finishes

Switch RAL colours and finishes so buyers see the configuration they would order.

Shareable views

Every product and part has its own link. Sales can send a buyer straight to the right detail.

Two languages

English and Chinese built in. Adding a language means adding text, not code.

Trade shows

The same build runs your stand.

Switch on kiosk mode and the screen plays the walkthroughs by itself. When someone touches it, it stops and hands over control. When they walk away, it starts again.

  • Attract loop

    Cycles through every product's walkthrough and stops the moment someone touches the screen.

  • Take-home QR code

    The code on screen follows the product and part on display. Visitors scan it and the same view opens on their phone.

  • Runs offline

    The offline build carries its models, lighting and fonts. A poor venue connection doesn't stop the demo.

  • Counts stand visitors

    Each visitor session is recorded: how long they stayed and which products and parts they looked at.

FAQ

What people usually ask.

Does it work on phones?

Yes. Phones get a lighter version of each model, and the renderer adapts to the device. It is tested on iPhone Safari, where heavy 3D pages often fail.

Can it run at a show without internet?

Yes. The offline build includes every model, lighting file and font. Enquiries and analytics wait in a queue and are sent when the connection comes back.

Can it carry our brand?

Yes. The name, logo, colours and the light-box signs in the 3D scene all come from one brand folder, so a white-label build is a configuration change.

Where is it hosted?

It is a static web app: it can run on our hosting, on your server or on any CDN, and it can be embedded in your existing website.

What happens to our CAD data?

Only an optimised display model reaches the browser, not your engineering files. Products that must not be public can be left out of the public build and shown only at shows or in sales meetings.

What does it cost?

It depends on how many products you need and how much each one should do. Tell us about one product and we will come back with a quote.
